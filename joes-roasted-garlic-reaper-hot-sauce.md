# Joe's Roasted Garlic Reaper Hot Sauce

**Status:** Successful first batch; baseline recipe (v1)  
**Source:** Joe's recipe and process, shared November 2026  
**Inspired by:** Silagy Sauce Roasted Garlic Reaper

Joe developed this after trying Silagy's Roasted Garlic Reaper hot sauce. The result is not an exact clone, but Joe, Scott, and Susana liked it; Susana especially enjoyed it. Preserve this first implementation as the reference batch before changing variables.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| White onion, roughly chopped | 1 |
| Garlic cloves | About 7 |
| Carrot, whole and unpeeled | 1 |
| Ghost peppers | 7 |
| White vinegar | Approximately 3/4 cup |
| Water | Approximately 3/4 cup |
| Kosher salt | Approximately 1 tsp |
| Olive oil | Some, for the onions |

All amounts above are from Joe's original description; they were not measured more precisely.

## Process (Joe's v1)

1. Get a gas grill hot. Roast the whole carrot until soft and charred. Remove it and peel off the charred exterior.
2. Heat a cast-iron skillet until ripping hot. Roast the chopped onion in some olive oil until it develops color.
3. Add the whole garlic cloves and develop color on them as well. Remove the onion and garlic and set aside.
4. Remove the stems from seven ghost peppers.
5. Put the peppers in a blender with the roasted carrot, onion, garlic, approximately 1 teaspoon kosher salt, water, and vinegar. Blend until smooth.
6. Simmer the puree in a saucepan for 10–15 minutes.
7. Optionally strain for a finer, more liquid consistency.
8. Refrigerate and let sit for 24 hours before serving.

**Unspecified in the original:** grill and skillet temperatures, roasting durations, sizes/weights of vegetables and peppers, final yield, whether the first batch was strained, and finished acidity/pH.

## Candidate follow-up experiments (not part of v1)

- **Pepper treatment:** Joe's recipe blends raw ghost peppers; try fire-roasting them before blending to test the effect on flavor.
- **Measurements:** Weigh the prepared peppers, onion, carrot, garlic, vinegar, water, and salt; record the sauce yield.
- **Flavor balance:** Compare small, controlled changes to vinegar/water ratio, onion-to-carrot ratio, and garlic roasting.
- **Texture:** Compare strained and unstrained samples.
- **Repeatability:** Document the roast times, finished pH, heat perception, and tasting results after the 24-hour rest.

Change one variable at a time where practical. Keep the original v1 unchanged as the control.

## Handling and storage

Ghost peppers are extremely hot: wear gloves and avoid breathing in pepper vapors while blending and simmering; ventilate the kitchen. Treat this as a **refrigerated, perishable sauce**, not a shelf-stable canned recipe. The unspecified final pH and short simmer are not sufficient to establish safe room-temperature storage.
